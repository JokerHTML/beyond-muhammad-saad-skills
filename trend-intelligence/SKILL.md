# Trend Intelligence

Produce a weekly, evidence-tagged scan of what is changing across your territory (AI tools, audience questions, creator/freelancer problems, platform changes) so your content plan is fed by current reality, not recycled ideas. Every candidate is separated into FACT / SIGNAL / SPECULATION and scored with an angle for your offer — never blind trend-chasing.

**Who it is for:** creators, freelancers and small digital-product businesses who publish weekly and need to know what moved since last week — and what of it is actually worth making content about.

**What it is not:** a news aggregator, a social-listening dashboard, or a content calendar. It does one job: a weekly scan that ends in a ranked top 5 with one usable angle each. It never writes the content itself; it feeds the builder/writer, which has its own QC gates.

## Evidence labels — read before quoting anything

- **FACT** — verified against an official source (docs, changelog, announcement, help page), OR the same claim stated by **two independent sources**. One source alone is never a FACT, however reputable.
- **REPORTED** — a named study, report or reputable secondary source, quoted with its source, date and caveats. A single study counts here even when it is primary research — if the authors have a stake in the answer, say so.
- **SIGNAL** — indicative but weak: community reports, a single anecdote, a viral post, a pattern in comments. Evidence of a reported experience, never of a universal fact.
- **SPECULATION** — unverified, or sources conflict. Never presented as fact, never enters content claims. Parked, not published.
- **Recommendation** — a working rule derived from the evidence, not evidence itself.

## When to use

- Weekly — suggested Monday, so the week's content plan can absorb it.
- On-demand before monthly/quarterly planning, or when a major launch/shift breaks (new model release, platform policy change, payment-rule change).

## Inputs

None required. Optional, and worth setting once:

- **Focus areas** for this week (e.g. "dig into AI video tools", "check what freelancers complain about on X").
- **Your lanes** — the topics you track. Defaults: AI tool updates + new AI features · audience questions · creator problems · freelancer problems · content-creation problems · automation · digital products / online business · platform changes (the platforms you publish on). Delete lanes you do not serve; add lanes you do.
- **Your offer** — the product or service the scan must feed (one line: who buys it, what problem it solves). Ranking is relevance to *this* buyer pool.
- **Watchlist** — 5–10 terms you track every week (tools, competitors, topics). See step 5.

## Process

### 1. Set the window and stamp every source

- Scan window: the last 7 days for the top 5; the last 30 days for the rest of the file. Anything older than 30 days is excluded unless it is a standing fact (a rule, a limit, a policy) — and then it is labelled standing, with its date.
- **As-of timestamp on every source snapshot.** A board, a leaderboard or a search page is only useful if you record when you read it. A week-old board read as if it were live is how a scan invents movement that never happened. Record the read date and time beside every source.
- If last week's scan file exists, open it first. This week's scan is a *delta* against it, not a fresh list.

### 2. Scan the lanes

Use free tools only — no API keys, no paid subscriptions (see "Free-tools ladder" below). For each lane, collect candidates; do not filter yet. A candidate is a *change*: something launched, broke, shifted, got asked more, or got complained about — not a standing topic with no news.

### 3. Record every candidate with the same 10 fields

TOPIC · SOURCE (URL + read date) · DATE (when it happened, not when you found it) · WHY IT IS CURRENT · AUDIENCE PROBLEM (whose problem, in their words where possible) · YOUR ANGLE (the one content angle for your offer) · CONTENT FORMAT (post / carousel / reel / short video / thread / long-form — one, not "any") · PRODUCT RELEVANCE (high / medium / low, against your offer's buyer pool) · CONFIDENCE (high / medium / low) · **SENTIMENT** (hype / scepticism / frustration / neutral — what the audience feels about it, not what you feel) · **CORROBORATION** (how many independent sources say it: 1 / 2 / 3+).

A candidate mentioned by 3+ independent sources gets a corroboration bump in ranking; a candidate with one source and no corroboration cannot rank above medium confidence, whatever it claims.

### 4. Classify every candidate

- **FACT** — official source, or two independent sources saying the same thing. Every FACT needs a citable source with a date. No source, no FACT.
- **REPORTED** — one named study/report/secondary source, quoted with date and caveats.
- **SIGNAL** — community reports, single anecdotes, viral posts. Use it to identify what should be *tested*, never as a claim.
- **SPECULATION** — unverified or conflicting. Never presented as fact. Parked in the scan file under "watched, no angle yet" — never forced into a product connection.

### 5. Watchlist delta — what changed since last scan

For each watchlist term, record the same query run the same way as last week and compare endpoint values, never averages: this week's reading vs last week's reading, with both dates. A slope gets a number instead of an impression. Then write one line per term: rising / flat / falling / dead, and whether the move changes anything you will make. New terms join the watchlist only after they survive one full scan as a FACT or REPORTED candidate.

### 6. Rank

Rank by **PRODUCT RELEVANCE × CONFIDENCE**. A high-confidence topic with no product relevance still loses to a medium-confidence topic that feeds your offer's buyer pool. Tie-breakers, in order: corroboration count (3+ beats 1), then freshness (≤7 days beats older), then sentiment strength (frustration and scepticism are stronger content fuel than hype — people act on problems, not excitement).

### 7. Output the top 5–10

Each with the single best content angle and the recommended format. Then the full scan file, then the briefing (below). A trend with no product angle is parked under "watched, no angle yet" — never force a product connection.

## The gate — every trend passes it before entering the plan

1. **IS IT REAL?** — FACT or REPORTED, or a SIGNAL strong enough to test. SPECULATION fails here.
2. **IS IT CURRENT?** — inside the window, or a standing fact with a date. Last month ≠ today.
3. **IS IT RELEVANT?** — to your offer's buyer pool, not to the internet in general.
4. **CAN WE ADD ORIGINAL VALUE?** — a genuinely new angle, test, or experience — not a rewrite of someone else's take.
5. **DOES IT FIT?** — your niche, your voice, your channels.

A "no" on any = park it. The scan proposes; the plan disposes.

## Free-tools ladder — $0, no keys, in order

1. **Web search with the matching verticals** (`news` for launches and policy changes, `datetime` for dates) — text queries, never bare URLs. Date-filter where the tool allows it.
2. **Google Trends** — free, no key. Use it for the watchlist slope (step 5): same term, same region, week-over-week endpoint values. It measures search interest, not truth — a rising term is a signal, not a fact.
3. **Official sources first** — docs, changelogs, announcements, help pages. Open them and read the claim in full; a summary page is a lead, not a source.
4. **Platform-native search** — search the platform itself (TikTok search, YouTube search, Reddit search) for the candidate term and inspect the top results: exact-match titles, big channels or small ones, dates. That two-minute check is the real competition/demand score. No platform publishes search volumes, so no numeric volume appears in a scan.
5. **Social search** — community signal (Reddit, X, Threads chatter). SIGNAL only, never FACT.
6. **Creator Search Insights / Studio Research tabs** (where your accounts have them) — what your own audience searches for, and where content is missing. Free, in-app, audience-specific.

Source hierarchy, always: official docs > official announcements > primary research > reputable independent tests > community signal. Random summaries are leads, not sources.

## Quality rules

- Never turn Reddit/community complaints into universal facts. A complaint is evidence of a reported experience; use it to identify what should be tested.
- Verify time-sensitive claims at scan time: re-check AI tool features, platform rules, pricing and fees. Last month ≠ today.
- Never quote a number you did not verify: no invented search volumes, no invented growth percentages, no invented "X% of creators" figures. If a figure would strengthen a line and you do not have one, write the line without it.
- A viral post is one data point. A pattern is 3+ independent mentions. Do not promote the first to the second.
- Do not chase a trend your offer cannot serve. The scan's job includes saying "watched, no angle yet".

## Failure modes

- **Stale sources presented as current.** Mitigate: date-check every source during the scan; the 30-day filter is a hard rule, not a guideline.
- **Hype without evidence.** Mitigate: FACT / SIGNAL / SPECULATION tags are mandatory on every candidate; anything untagged is rejected.
- **Trend with no product angle.** Mitigate: park it under "watched, no angle yet" — never force a product connection.
- **One source, treated as consensus.** Mitigate: corroboration count is a recorded field; a single-source candidate cannot be high-confidence.
- **Watchlist drift.** Mitigate: same query, same way, every week; endpoint values + dates recorded, never averaged.

## Output

### Scan file

Dated file `trend-scan-YYYY-MM-DD.md` in your research folder, with:

1. Header: scan date, window (last 7 / 30 days), lanes scanned, watchlist terms checked.
2. **Watchlist delta** — one line per term (rising / flat / falling / dead + endpoint values + dates).
3. **Ranked candidates** — top 5–10 with all 10 fields + tag each.
4. **Watched, no angle yet** — parked trends with the reason they were parked.
5. **Speculation log** — unverified/conflicting claims, kept out of content.
6. **Sources** — every URL with its read date.

### Top-5 briefing (chat)

Concise, no fluff — per topic: topic + why it matters now + the single best angle + format. Nothing else. The briefing is the deliverable the plan acts on; the file is the audit trail.

## Verification

- Spot-check 2–3 sources per scan by re-opening them and confirming the claim still reads the same.
- The plan owner sanity-checks the angles before any enter the content plan — the scan proposes, the plan disposes.

## Stop conditions

- Stop if key sources are paywalled or blocked with no accessible alternative — report the gap, do not invent content to fill it.
- Stop if fewer than 3 verifiable candidates emerge — deliver a thin-but-honest scan rather than padding with speculation.
- Never publish or schedule content from the scan; it feeds the builder/writer, which has its own QC and approval gates.

## Sources and comparables (23 Nov 2026)

Best existing skills for the same job, compared before writing this one:

- **last30days (brianrwagner)** — the closest comparable: 30-day freshness filter, Reddit/X/web synthesis, pattern detection (3+ mentions), sentiment, watchlists, briefings. Requires Brave Search API + Bird CLI + optional reddit-insights MCP — API-key heavy. Adopted: the 30-day hard filter, the 3+ corroboration rule, the sentiment field. Not adopted: the API-key setup — this skill runs on free tools only.
- **Trend Radar** — real-time aggregation across 7 platforms, zero-config, daily briefings, progressive disclosure. Adopted: the briefing-first shape (top 5 up front, full file behind it). Not adopted: daily cadence — weekly is the rhythm that feeds a content plan without noise.
- **Trends MCP weekly scan** — fixed growth windows, as-of timestamps on every board, slope as a number. Adopted: as-of timestamps and the watchlist-delta method (endpoint values, never averages).
- **SourceWeft trend-discovery** — ranks evidence, writes a markdown brief, clusters by topic/format/hook. Requires a paid ScrapeCreators API key. Adopted: evidence ranking over raw views. Not adopted: the paid dependency.
- **Niche Research (charlie947/social-media-skills)** — browser-driven, 7-day freshness guarantee, 20-theme synthesis, shareable angles. Adopted: the ≤7-day freshness bar for the top 5.
- **samber deep-research** — two-independent-sources rule for critical claims, confidence tracking, gaps stated. Adopted: the two-source FACT bar.
- **daily.dev developer-trends-ideas** — reads one free weekly source (daily.dev) and turns it into post/video ideas. Adopted: the "one free source can anchor a scan" principle — the free-tools ladder above generalises it.
- **askill trend-jacking (vivy-yi)** — Xiaohongshu-focused trend leverage. No transferable method beyond "ride the trend with your angle", which the gate already covers.

**Net finding:** no comparable skill combines a weekly cadence with mandatory evidence tags, a watchlist delta, a product-relevance ranking and a $0 tool stack. The API-key skills are stronger on automation; this one is stronger on honesty and cost. Nothing was copied wholesale.
