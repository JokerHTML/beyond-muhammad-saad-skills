# YouTube Shorts SEO

Build every Short's SEO package so it can be found in Search and survive the Shorts feed: one keyword phrase, one title, one description, tags, hashtags, hook, thumbnail, first comment, cadence. This skill covers the **text package only**. Sourcing, permission, editing, music and account safety live in your production skill — this skill never replaces them.

## Evidence labels — read before quoting anything

- **FACT** — YouTube's own Help text, re-verified live 23 Nov 2026, answer ID named.
- **REPORTED** — a named study or secondary source, with date, sample size and caveats. Observational, not an experiment.
- **UNVERIFIED** — no source found. Never quote as fact.
- **Recommendation** — a working rule derived from the evidence, not evidence itself.

YouTube publishes no search volumes, no ranking weights and no ideal video length. Treat any exact ranking percentage or exact monthly search volume quoted anywhere as invented. Nothing in this skill invents a number: if a figure would strengthen a line and you do not have one, write the line without it.

## 0. The honest tension — read this first

**SEO is the smaller door.** (REPORTED, Metricool 2026: 799,718 videos, 71,177 accounts.) Shorts views by source in that study: feed 61.5% · subscribers 11.3% · search 8.8% · Related 5.8% · playlist 5.1% · channel 3.1% · hashtags 0.03%. Caveat: whether that split is Shorts-only or all formats is disputed — HypeNest (2026) reads the same Metricool data as all-format. Treat the split as directional, not a Shorts-only fact. Either way, a perfect SEO package fights over roughly a tenth of views. The hook and retention fight over the rest. Do the SEO properly, then spend the remaining effort on the first 3 seconds.

**SEO cannot fix the originality risk.** (FACT, YouTube Community, 1 Oct 2026 Shorts originality update.) Recommendations prioritise original content and reduce reach of content re-uploaded from other creators "without adding anything of your own". A credit line in the description does NOT satisfy this — transformation must be in the video (hook, subtitles, curation, editing; narration for compilations). Never present SEO as protection against a reused-content flag or a Content ID claim. (REPORTED, Revid 2026: a Short over 1 minute with any active Content ID claim is blocked worldwide.)

**Spelling: title uses the official name, everywhere else carries the search spelling.** The search-dominant spelling of a game is often not its official one (e.g. searchers type "GTA 6", the official title is "GTA VI"). Rule: the **title uses the official spelling** — it is the brand decision and it stands; do not "fix" it to chase search. The search spelling is carried where the rules allow it: description synonyms, the Tags field, and hashtags. See Appendix A for the worked example.

## 1. Keyword first — one phrase per Short

1. **Pick one target phrase before writing anything.** Format: [Creator/Character] + [Game] + [specific moment], e.g. "[Creator] [Game] train" — never a bare game name, never a bare genre word.
2. **Verify it by searching it signed out; inspect the top 10.** Are they exact-match titles? Big channels or small ones? Old videos? That two-minute check is the real competition score. YouTube publishes no search volumes, so no numeric volume appears in this skill.
3. **Free tools, in order:** YouTube autocomplete (type the topic slowly, write down the suggestions — they are real searches, ranked by frequency) → YouTube Studio Research tab (what your viewers search, where content is missing) → Google Trends with the YouTube Search filter → vidIQ / TubeBuddy Keyword Explorer for scores. (Recommendation; Revid 2026.)
4. **Tier method.** Sort candidate phrases into three tiers before you commit to a calendar:
   - **Tier 1 — attack first:** proven demand, winnable competition (small channels hold top spots, exact-match titles are thin). One specific moment per Short.
   - **Tier 2 — build toward:** demand proven, competition harder, or format mismatch (compilation-sized topics, guide terms). Use as second keyword, not the lead title phrase.
   - **Tier 3 — do not lead with:** saturated, dead-end, expired, or wrong-format terms. Never a Tier 3 term as the lead.
   - Appendix A shows the method applied to GTA VI. The tiers are the method; the lists are one dated example.
5. **The query test (adopted from yt-seo).** Before handing the package over, write the **three search queries this Short should win** and check the title and first two description lines contain the words in those queries. If they do not, the problem is the title, not the description.

## 2. Titles

### 2.1 Hard limits (FACT)

- Title limit: **100 characters**. Mobile truncates around 60–70 chars in the feed; roughly 40 chars show in the Shorts feed overlay.
- Put the most important words near the beginning; save episode numbers and branding for the end (Help, "Thumbnail & title tips").
- Title must accurately represent the video — if not, viewers stop watching, "which can impact discoverability" (Help). No "full stream", "full gameplay", no moment absent from the clip. (VERIFIED, Spam Policy: a title offering a full sports match on a clip is YouTube's own example of misleading metadata.)

### 2.2 Length target (REPORTED — two studies disagree, so the target is their overlap)

- **Aim 30–60 characters, hook + main keyword inside the first 50.** OverseerOS (18,436 Shorts, Oct 2026) found 20–40 best, but the within-channel ratio was only 1.04× — length alone barely matters. Returbe (63,480 videos, Nov 2026) found gaming best at 41–70 chars (8.6% outliers); under 40: 8.4%; over 70: 7.7%; 90+ consistently underperforms (AIR, 18,080 channels). AIR also found under-30 wins in Gaming/Entertainment on curiosity gaps.
- **Rule: never pad a title to reach a length band, and never butcher a specific title to hit 40.** A title that needs 41–60 chars to name the specific moment is fine. Specificity beats length.

### 2.3 What works (REPORTED, Returbe 63,480 videos — outlier = 3× channel median, average 11.1%)

- Open a gap: something the viewer doesn't know, a claim to check, someone who "has no idea" (**22.0%** outliers — best pattern measured).
- "Why" + a fact: **14.7%**. "Never" / "nobody": **14.7%**. One word in quotes: **14.4%**.
- A number at the START (12.8%) beats a number anywhere (10.6%). Hype words describing something concrete (13.4%) beat hype as decoration.
- Below average — avoid: hashtag in the title (**8.0%**, worst habit measured), whole title in CAPS (9.0%), exclamation marks (9.2%), ellipsis "…" (9.3%), question marks (9.6% — turn the question into a "Why" statement), emojis (9.6%, no effect), "How to" (9.7%).
- Curiosity language ("what nobody tells you", "the real reason") is the only positive signal that transfers across all niches and channel sizes (AIR).
- At most one of each: one bracketed element, one number, one emoji (at the end), one ALL-CAPS word, 1–2 power words. YouTube's own line is "Limit ALL CAPS and emoji" (FACT).

### 2.4 Title formulas — 8, deduped from 16 across the sources

All formulas keep the official game spelling first or early, creator named, 30–60 chars, statement not question, no "!!!", no "...", no hashtag, no emoji in the title. The moment word is the hook; the creator name is the search anchor. Examples below are drawn from the labelled GTA VI worked example (Appendix A/B): game = GTA VI, creator names (Speed, ohnePixel, Lucia) are example clip subjects, not required names — substitute your own game and creator.

**F1 — Moment + reaction:** `[Game]: [Creator] [does X] — [reaction]`
- "GTA VI: Speed's Red Ferrari Tears Through Vice City" (51)
- "GTA VI: ohnePixel Tries to Stop the Train" (41)

**F2 — Number + stakes (incl. Top N lists):** `[Game]: [Creator] [number] [thing] [outcome]`
- "GTA VI: Lucia Checks Her $532K Bank Balance" (43)
- "GTA VI: Top 5 Endings Ranked Worst to Best" (42) — list format; the number must be real and countable in the video.

**F3 — "Has no idea" gap:** `[Game]: [Creator] Has No Idea [what's about to happen]`
- "GTA VI: Speed Has No Idea the Train Is Coming" (45)
- Use sparingly (1 in 5 titles max) — the pattern is strong precisely because it is rare.

**F4 — "Why" + fact:** `[Game]: Why [Creator] [did the surprising thing]`
- "GTA VI: Why Speed Jumped Off the Bridge" (39)
- Only when the clip actually shows the reason — never invent one.

**F5 — Absolute word:** `[Game]: [Creator] Never [expected X] / Nobody [expected X]`
- "GTA VI: Nobody Expected Lucia to Do This" (40)
- The claim must be checkable in the clip itself.

**F6 — Quoted word:** `[Game]: [Creator] Calls It "[word]" — [moment]`
- "GTA VI: Speed Calls It 'The Worst Ending Ever'" (46)
- Only quote words actually said in the clip (transcript-verified).

**F7 — Series + episode:** `[Game] [SERIES] - #[N] ([Genre])`
- "GTA 6 FAILS - #1 (GTA 6 Funny Moments)" (38) — note: this series format uses the search spelling; see §0. Number only after 3+ Shorts exist in the series.

**F8 — One-sentence moment (plain, specific, countable):** `[Game]: [Character] [did X]`
- "GTA VI: Lucia Falls Through the Map" (35)
- "GTA VI: NPC Walks a Pet Alligator" (33)
- One specific, countable thing per title. Never a generic comparison, never a star count resting on a leak, never a review score as the hook.

**Title gate:** pre-write 2–3 variants per Short; pick one, keep one spare. Rotate formulas — never run the same formula twice in a row. Read the title aloud: if a viewer who clicked for exactly what it promises would feel shortchanged, rewrite it. That is the clickbait test.

## 3. Description

### 3.1 Structure — Template A (Shorts; fill [brackets])

| Line | Content | Character guidance |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The moment, in one sentence.** Game + creator + what happens + where. The 1–2 main words included. | **80–120 chars.** The whole point must fit inside ~100 chars — YouTube publishes no visible count for the Shorts player (estimates conflict: ~100 / 100–125 / ~150); the title + up to 3 hashtags show, the rest hides behind "more". |
| 2 | **One supporting detail sentence** — the specific beat (character, place, reaction). Different words from Line 1, same topic. Adds search synonyms without stuffing. | **60–100 chars** |
| 3 | **Credit (one line).** `Credit: [Creator] — [original stream/video link as plain text]` | ~45–70 chars. Attribution norm; the link is NOT clickable in a Shorts description (see §3.2), so keep it short. |
| 4 | **Subscribe CTA (one line, locked).** `Subscribe for a new [Game] clip every day.` | ~40–50 chars. Single ask; never the first line. Fill [Game] with the game's name (e.g. "GTA VI"). |
| 5 | **Disclaimer block (locked).** `Disclaimer: Gameplay from [Game full title] © [Rights holder]. Stream moment by [Creator]. All rights belong to their respective owners. This video is shared for entertainment purposes only. [Channel] is not affiliated with [Rights holder] or [Creator]. Contact: [contact email]` | Fixed text; creator, game, channel and email slots change per video. Placement: after the subscribe line, before the hashtag line. |
| 6 | **Hashtag line (locked format, §5).** `#[Game] #[GameVariant] #[Topic] #[Creator] #[Moment]` | 3–5 tags, never more than 6 total. |

**Do NOT add to a Short:** a links list (not clickable), chapters/timestamps (a ~50-second clip has no sections), a keyword / "related searches" block, >6 hashtags, or a business email outside the disclaimer block.

### 3.2 Rules — what helps, what hurts

- **Write the description for search, not for the feed.** (FACT, Help answer/12948449 + answer/11914225.) Descriptions tell the algorithm and viewers what the video is about; search terms are matched against description text. The Shorts feed runs on watch/skip behaviour (chose to view, avg view duration, avg % viewed, likes, post-watch surveys) — description text is NOT on YouTube's feed-ranking list.
- **Description hard cap: 5,000 characters.** (FACT, answer/12948449.) Same limit for Shorts as long-form. Practical target for a ~50-second Short: lines 1–4 + hashtag line ~300–450 chars; the disclaimer footer sits below that and does not count toward the target.
- **Feature 1–2 main words prominently in BOTH title and description.** (FACT, answer/12948449.) "Pretend you're searching for your video — what would you type into the search bar?"
- **Every video needs a UNIQUE description.** (FACT, answer/12948449.) Lines 1–2 must be fresh every video. The footer (credit + subscribe + disclaimer + hashtags) may repeat verbatim — YouTube RECOMMENDS the default-description field for boilerplate on every upload (FACT). Set the locked footer once in Studio as the default description; write only lines 1–2 fresh per video.
- **Keywords go in sentences, never in a wall.** (FACT, Help answer/6390658: "adding ordinary descriptive tags or repetitive sentences in the description is (still) prohibited" — video may be removed or penalised.) The frequently quoted tags-page sentence about "excessive tags" was NOT in the Tags page text fetched 20 Nov 2026 — treat that exact wording as REPORTED, never quote it as verified. Either way: a comma-separated keyword block is the prohibited pattern.
- **Shorts description links are NOT clickable.** (FACT, Help answer/13748639: "URLs placed in YouTube Shorts comments and Shorts descriptions are non-clickable.") Mentions and hashtags in Shorts descriptions ARE clickable; the Related Video link IS clickable. Never build a Shorts CTA around a description link. The credit link is attribution, not a click path. Long-form description links ARE clickable.
- **Hashtags: 3–5, at the end.** (FACT, answer/6390658: up to 3 description hashtags — YouTube's most-engaging pick — appear by the title; **>60 hashtags = YouTube ignores every hashtag** on the video, and over-tagging may remove the video from uploads/search; misleading/unrelated hashtags may result in removal.) YouTube gives no recommended count; 3–5 is secondary consensus (REPORTED). Hashtag pages drove 0.03% of Shorts views (REPORTED, Metricool — see §0 caveat) — their value is classification + the up-to-3 clickable tags by the title, not traffic.
- **Never describe a clip as something it is not.** (FACT, Spam Policy, "malicious clickbait".) A 50-second clip is never "the full stream", "full gameplay", or a moment that is not in the clip.
- **Disclaimers carry zero legal weight.** (REPORTED, LegalClarity 2026: "Disclaimers in your description do nothing… carry zero legal weight. Fair use depends on how you actually use the content, not what you write about it"; "Giving credit is not the same as fair use"; Content ID scans audio/video fingerprints and "doesn't read your description.") The disclaimer block is a good-faith signal + a contact path for credit/removal requests — never claim it protects against Content ID claims or strikes. Never claim permission you do not have.
- **Creator-only rule.** The package names ONLY the creator whose clip it is — no other creator, no other name — anywhere in title, description, tags, hashtags or first comment. A repeated footer is fine; a repeated description is not. A fully repeated description forfeits the search benefit and, with near-identical clips, looks like the templated/mass-produced pattern (see §0 originality risk).
- **Fix a bad description by editing it in place. Never delete and re-upload to fix copy.** (UNVERIFIED harm from edits; no YouTube statement, no views-based study of description-only edits found.) Deletion destroys views, comments, watch time and history, and the identical file risks the scraped/repetitive rules. The only description-edit study found (REPORTED, Peec AI via Search Engine Land, 6 Nov 2026, ~60 videos, 15 updated): +359% daily AI retrievals for updated videos vs +17% unchanged — observational, small, short window, measured AI retrievals NOT views. Do NOT transfer the TubeAI title study (572,365 videos, median −6.3% view pace after a title-only change) to descriptions — it is about TITLES only. Log every edit with timestamp + before/after.

### 3.3 Template B — Long-form compilation description (fill [brackets]; ~800–1,500 chars)

```
[Hook: what the compilation is + the best moment + "wait for the last one" tease, if true — 100–150 chars]
[Summary: 2–3 sentences — whose stream, which game/session, what kinds of moments. Repeat the 1–2 main words naturally.]
0:00 [Moment one — real start time]
0:46 [Moment two — real start time, at least 10 seconds after the previous one]
1:26 [Moment three — real start time]
…
Clips featured (full credit): [Creator] — [link]
All clips belong to their respective creators — contact for credit/removal.
[Comment question: "Which moment got you?"]
Subscribe for a new [Game] compilation every day.
#[Game] #[GameVariant] #[Topic] #[Creator] #[MomentKeyword]
For commercial inquiries: [contact email]
```

Notes: chapters need ≥3 timestamps, first at 0:00, each ≥10 seconds apart (secondary guides — UNVERIFIED as a YouTube rule; use real start times, never duplicated placeholders). Long-form description links ARE clickable. Email sits at the bottom only. A compilation is safe ONLY with your own voiceover/narration + a story structure (ranking/explaining, not just joining clips) + short segments + cleared creators first — credits alone do not make it safe (see §0).

## 4. Tags (Tags field)

- **Count: 5–10 tags. 2 minutes, then stop.** (House practice reconciled: one source used 10, the other 5–8. YouTube publishes no ideal count.) 10 tags of 2–3 words ≈ 150–250 chars, well inside the 500-char total.
- **Tags play a minimal role in discovery.** (FACT, Help answer/146402: "Tags can be useful if the content of your video is commonly misspelled. Otherwise, tags play a minimal role in your video's discovery." + "Your video's title, thumbnail, and description are more important pieces of metadata.") If a tag set is weak, the fix is the title and the hook, not more tags. Five of the top short videos read 21 Nov 2026 used ZERO tags — including two 2M+ clips.
- **Tags: 500 characters total, all tags combined.** (REPORTED, YouTube Data API docs.) YouTube publishes no maximum number of tags.
- **Order:** (1) most-searched specific phrase for THIS moment → (2) broader variants → (3) misspellings / alternate spellings → (4) channel tags last. Order is housekeeping — YouTube publishes no tag-order weighting (UNVERIFIED).
- **Mix per video:** 3–4 moment-specific · 2–3 game/topic · 1–2 creator · 2–3 channel-name tags. Channel tags are the one intentional repeat.
- **Spelling:** carry BOTH the official and the search-dominant spelling across the set (e.g. "[game] 6 ferrari" + "[game] vi driving"). Tags are where the channel covers the search spelling.
- **Fresh every time:** content tags are never repeated from another video. Before each upload, run the trend refresh (§11) and the top videos for the moment, then write fresh tags for THIS clip. Never paste a big channel's tag list verbatim.
- **Never paste tags into the description.** (§3.2 — spam policy.) The hidden Tags field and the description are separate systems.

## 5. Hashtags — one owner: this section

- **Formula: 3–5 tags, never more than 6 total.** `#[Game-no-space] #[Game-variant] #[Topic] #[Creator] #[Moment]` — one broad topic tag, the rest about the exact subject. (REPORTED, Revid 2026: 3–5 specific ones; TikTok data in the same study points the same way, 3–4 best median — TikTok data, not YouTube, directional only.)
- **The creator tag is mandatory — and it must be THE clip's creator.** A fixed creator hashtag on every video contradicts the creator-only rule (§3.2) and is a defect this skill fixes: never tag a creator who is not in the video. The moment tag must be a word genuinely in the clip (e.g. #[Game]Train, #[Game]Drunk).
- **Hard cliff: >60 hashtags = ALL hashtags ignored.** (FACT, answer/6390658.) The "15" figure is stale (pre-2021 archived page) — never use it.
- **Do NOT put #Shorts in the title.** (REPORTED, OverseerOS 17,280-Shorts study: within-channel ratio 1.01× — no consistent advantage; current Help pages do not mention #Shorts at all; YouTube classifies a Short by format, not hashtag.) If used at all, #Shorts goes 5th or 6th in the description, never displacing a topic tag — or omit it entirely.
- **Hashtags are NOT a Shorts ranking signal.** (REPORTED: YouTube's Shorts ranking inputs are % chose to view, avg view duration, avg % viewed, likes, post-watch surveys; hashtags are not on the list.) Do not cite the "28% impression boost from 3–5 hashtags" figure — its primary source was not located (UNVERIFIED).

## 6. Hooks — first 3 seconds + first description line

### 6.1 The video hook (the feed decides)

- **The two numbers that decide a Short:** Viewed vs. Swiped Away + Average View Duration (REPORTED, vidIQ). Check both in Studio Analytics before changing anything else.
- **Viewers decide in ~1 second** (REPORTED, Jenny Hoyos, YouTube Blog Jan 2025). First frame + first sentence carry the whole video.
- **A Short cut from a long video must survive without the video around it** (adopted from yt-shorts): it opens on a complete thought, contains a turn (a claim, then something that complicates or proves it), and ends on a line, not a trail-off. Write a NEW first line for the Short — the long video's line assumes context this viewer does not have.
- **Three patterns that stop the swipe** (REPORTED, Fliki/vidIQ/Teleprompter 2026):
  1. **Pattern interrupt** — open mid-action: face mid-reaction, bright colour flash, jump-cut to the most unusual frame. No logo, no slow zoom, no establishing shot, no "welcome back".
  2. **Promise gap** — state an outcome, open a small information gap ("The reason the plan fell apart"). The brain hates an open loop.
  3. **Contrarian opener** — a sentence against common belief. For clip channels the creator's disbelief IS the opener.
- **Say the search phrase in the first sentence and show it on screen.** Spoken + on-screen text in the opening seconds is a search signal and serves muted viewers. (FACT, answer/16090438: relevance weighs "how well the title, tags, description, and video content match your search query".) Verify subtitle words against the transcript.
- **Length: as long as the topic needs, no longer.** (REPORTED, Revid 2026: length alone barely moves views; every length bucket landed within a narrow median band on TikTok data.) Test one shorter cut (25–30s) against your median, judged at 72h. Do not re-cut the whole queue on one study.
- **Loop-friendly ending:** the last frame should hand back to the first — replays multiply watch time without extra production. (Adopted from yt-shorts: a loop point — what the last line sets up so the first line answers it.)
- **Self-test:** mute the clip, watch only the first 2 seconds at thumb distance. If you would swipe past your own Short, the audience will too.

### 6.2 The description hook (search = the smaller door)

- L1 IS the description hook (§3.1). It must contain the moment in words a searcher would type: creator + game + what happens + where.
- L2 is the second hook: the specific beat (character, place, reaction) in different words — this is where the synonyms earn their search matches.

## 7. Thumbnails

- **Shorts: AUTO until the channel is verified.** Custom Shorts thumbnails can only be uploaded from a computer by verified accounts (REPORTED, Revid). Until verification is done, the first frame IS the thumbnail in the feed — frame 1 must carry the reaction + hook text, not a black frame or a menu screen. Do not delay an upload for a custom thumbnail.
- **When verification lands (upgrade, not blocker):** custom Shorts thumbnail 1080×1920 (recommended 2160×3840), 9:16, JPG/PNG under 2 MB, Studio on a computer. Face/reaction large + ≤5 words of bold text that **extend (not duplicate) the title** — title and thumbnail are one pairing; writing them separately is why half of a click surface says the same thing twice (adopted from yt-package).
- **Long-form (compilations):** 1280×720, 16:9, under 2 MB, JPG/PNG (FACT, Help). vidIQ's 500-breakout study (REPORTED, Jun 2026): 69% face, 56% high contrast, 89% at least one; face in 80% of the top 50; median overlay text 5 words. One focal point, 3–5 words, face ~¼–⅓ of frame, preview at ~320px wide, keep text out of the bottom-right badge zone and the edges.
- **CTR reality:** half of all videos sit at 2–10% CTR; gaming 8–15% (REPORTED, thumbn.so citing YouTube). YouTube optimises for expected watch time per impression, not CTR alone — a clickbait thumbnail that produces low watch time gets fewer recommendations. Judge Shorts by viewed-vs-swiped-away and avg % viewed — CTR is the wrong metric for Shorts (no thumbnail to click in the feed).

## 8. First comment + pin

- **What YouTube says (FACT, Help):** a channel can pin one comment per video (its own or a viewer's); the pinned comment shows first. Pinning a new one replaces the old one. Creator tips: pin your own comment to clarify something, ask a question, tease a moment or thank viewers. Pinning requires Advanced features (FACT, answer/6000964); until the gate opens, post the first comment anyway and pin it once the gate opens. A pinned comment cannot be set while a video is scheduled/private (REPORTED) — write the pin text in advance; post it the moment the video goes public.
- **How often top channels do it (REPORTED, MarkStudios, Oct 2026, 66 most-subscribed channels, 447 uploads):** 31% of regular videos, **14% of Shorts** had the creator's own comment first. Doing it every video is already above-median behaviour.
- **What works (REPORTED, small studies — directional):** a specific question beats a generic CTA. AIR (200 channels): well-made pinned comments +up to ~30% comment replies; polarising question +30–38%. No official statement links pinned comments to feed ranking (UNVERIFIED as a direct ranking effect). Do not cite unsourced "CTR" figures for pins.
- **House rules:** first comment posted **60 minutes after the video goes public — never 5 minutes after.** The comment is a specific question tied to the clip's moment (never a link, never a pitch, never a bare URL — Shorts comment links are non-clickable, FACT answer/13748639). Vary it per video — posting the same pinned text on every Short reads, on the face of the Spam Policy, as "repetitive comments … to drive … engagement". Never automate identical pins across a batch.
- **Heart + reply in the first hour, every video.** (REPORTED, CHI 2025 peer-reviewed study — Choi et al.: creator hearts soon after publishing incentivised more commenting over time; greatest effect within the first 5 hours.) A heart does NOT move a comment to the top (only pinning does).
- **First-comment formulas (one per video, moment-specific):**
  - Verdict: "Was it the worst ending ever or the perfect one? Drop your verdict."
  - Score: "Rate the flip — clean landing or total disaster? Drop your score."
  - Choice: "Take the gold or leave it? What's your call?"
  - Prediction: "Can you stop the train in [Game]? What should [Creator] test next?"
  - Wallet: "$532K in the bank — spend it or save it? What would you buy first?"

## 9. Cadence, timing, testing, and what NOT to chase

- **Cadence default: 1 strong Short/day, at your audience's peak hour.** (REPORTED, TrendReport 16 Nov 2026, 3.47M videos: 1/day **+8%** vs channel median; 2/day −4%; 3/day −5%; 4+/day **−11%**. Gaming: +7% / −5% / −1% / −11%.) Counter-evidence: Metricool 2026 found per-post best at 2–4/week (5,872 views). Treat 1/day as the default, not a law — judge by medians at 72h, not single-video spikes, and do not re-time the queue on a best-time blog post. (FACT, answer/11914225: YouTube says publish time does not affect long-term performance; best-time studies conflict.)
- **"First hour" myth:** no YouTube statement was found that the first hour decides distribution (UNVERIFIED). What vendor data supports: creator replies in the first 3 hours help comments (REPORTED, Astryx). The actionable part is being present in the comments, not posting at a magic minute.
- **No safe length exists** (UNVERIFIED). Only 3 min (Shorts line), 8 min (mid-rolls), 12 h (ceiling) are enforced. A 2–3 min compilation must be horizontal long-form, never vertical — a vertical video ≤3 min is a Short.
- **Test & Compare does NOT work on Shorts.** (FACT, Help answer/16391400: "You are not able to A/B test Shorts, Scheduled Lives, and Premiere videos at this time.") It covers titles AND thumbnails for long-form (up to 3 variants, winner by watch-time share, ≤2 weeks, desktop Studio only, Advanced features required). For Shorts, use the sequential protocol below.
- **Shorts testing protocol (Recommendation):** (a) test one variable at a time; (b) sequential pairs — publish Short A with variant 1, a different but comparable clip with variant 2 the next day at the same time; (c) minimum read 7 days per variant, ≥1,000 "shown in feed" per variant; call a winner only on a large gap (viewed-vs-swiped-away ≥5pp or avg % viewed ≥10pp); (d) never peek-and-stop early; (e) log everything in one sheet across 10–20 Shorts; (f) when traffic is <1K views/video, prefer pre-publish judgement over statistical testing. Compare a Short only against your own other Shorts.
- **Post-publish title edits — one edit max per Short, early (24–48h), small (2–5 words), never 3+ times.** (REPORTED, TubeAI 572,365-video study — correlation, not causation: −6.3% pace in 6h after a title-only change; 3+ retitles correlate with >2× flop rate.) No official rule says an edit hurts or resets a video; views/watch time are never reset by an edit. Never edit during a Test & Compare run. Log every edit with timestamp + before/after title. Never re-upload to fix copy.
- **Community posts: 2–3/week, <280 chars, polls that pick the next clip/ranking, image posts teasing the next Short, corrections/updates.** (REPORTED: the 500-subscriber rule was removed in 2023; eligibility is Standard vs Advanced — check Studio → Settings → Channel → Feature eligibility.) Do not budget Posts as a Shorts-view driver — no solid evidence they drive Shorts views; do not report any "% lift" figure as fact. Links in Posts are clickable only with Advanced features (REPORTED). Images must be owned/permissioned.
- **Captions:** correct the auto-caption track for every Short (Studio → Subtitles): fix proper nouns (creator names, game spelling as spoken), figures, misheard keywords. (REPORTED — case is transcript accuracy + sound-off retention + accessibility, not a ranking boost; whether YouTube indexes the caption transcript for Shorts search is UNVERIFIED.) Burned-in on-screen text is NOT a caption track.
- **Related Video link:** set it on every Short to the next clip / a playlist / a long-form compilation — the only in-player click path on a Short (REPORTED). It must be one of your own public/unlisted videos and needs Advanced features; if the gate is closed, log it as owed, do not skip silently. **Playlist rule:** every video goes into the playlist of the creator whose video it is — never another creator's playlist, never a generic playlist when a creator playlist exists.

## 10. The layered checklist — a failed gate stops the publish

Run Part A (description) first, then Part B (package), BEFORE upload. Then the post-publish verify. Every output ends in a copy-paste block and the question: **ship it, or change it?**

### Part A — Description gates (from the 13-gate description checklist)

| # | Step | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Main words** — 1–2 words (creator + game/moment) in title AND line 1. | Present in both. |
| D2 | **Line 1** — one sentence: game + creator + what happens + where. | 80–120 chars. Reads as a sentence, not a keyword list. |
| D3 | **Line 2** — one supporting detail sentence, different words from line 1. | 60–100 chars. Fresh — not copied from any other video. |
| D4 | **Creator check** — only the clip's creator named anywhere in the package. | No other creator, no other name. |
| D5 | **Credit** — creator + original stream/video link as plain text. | Present on every video. |
| D6 | **Subscribe CTA** — one line, never first. | Present, locked wording (§3.1 line 4). |
| D7 | **Disclaimer + contact** — locked block, slots filled. | Present on every video, after subscribe, before hashtags. |
| D8 | **Hashtags** — locked format + moment tag. | 3–5 tags, never more than 6, never >60. All directly related. Creator tag = this clip's creator. |
| D9 | **No tag wall** — no comma-separated keyword block, no "related searches" block. | None. Keywords live in sentences + the Tags field only. |
| D10 | **No misleading claim** — nothing promised that the clip does not contain. | No "full stream", "full gameplay", no moment absent from the clip. |
| D11 | **Uniqueness** — lines 1–2 differ from every other video's lines 1–2. | Fresh hook + context per video (FACT, answer/12948449). |
| D12 | **Length** — lines 1–4 + hashtag line ~300–450 chars; disclaimer footer excluded. | Well under the 5,000 hard cap. |
| D13 | **Post-publish fix** — if a description error is found after publish, edit in place. | Never re-upload to fix copy. Log the edit with timestamp + before/after. |

### Part B — Package gates

| # | Step | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Keyword + queries** — one target phrase, verified signed-out (§1 steps 1–2); three queries this Short should win (§1 step 5). | Tier 1 or a tested Tier 2 term. Never a Tier 3 term as the lead. Title + L1/L2 contain the query words. |
| P2 | **Title** — 2–3 variants written; one picked, one spare. | ≤100 chars (hard cap). Target 30–60. Keyword in first 3–5 words. Official game spelling. Statement, not question. No hashtag, no emoji, no "!!!", no "…", no ALL-CAPS title. Passes the clickbait test. |
| P3 | **Title formula** — one of §2.4 F1–F8, or a new formula with the same shape (gap + specificity + statement). | Note which formula was used — rotate them, never run the same formula twice in a row. |
| P4 | **Tags** — 5–10, fresh, both spellings covered. | ≤500 chars total. Order: specific → variants → misspellings → channel tags last. 2–3 channel tags (the one intentional repeat). Never pasted into the description. |
| P5 | **Hook** — first frame = reaction + hook text on screen; first sentence says the search phrase; subtitles burned in and transcript-checked. | Passes the muted 2-second self-test (§6.1). |
| P6 | **Thumbnail** — AUTO until verification, else custom. | First frame is the reaction + hook (AUTO), or custom 1080×1920 with ≤5 words that extend, not duplicate, the title. |
| P7 | **First comment** — specific question about THIS moment, written and queued. | Post at publish +60 min (never +5 min). No link, no pitch, no bare URL. |
| P8 | **Creator + playlist** — only the clip's creator named anywhere; video in that creator's playlist. | Passes D4 and §9 playlist rule. |
| P9 | **Length + format** — vertical 1080×1920, ≤3 min (a Short). | As long as the topic needs; a tested shorter cut where applicable. Copyright pre-screen done (listen-check any flagged music before upload). |
| P10 | **Captions + Related Video** — auto-captions corrected; Related Video link set. | Proper nouns correct. Link is one of your own public/unlisted videos, or logged as owed if Advanced features are closed. |
| P11 | **Post-publish verify** — read back title/description/tags/playlist/schedule from the details page (never the list view); open the Short as a viewer: title truncation (do the first 40 chars carry the moment?), thumbnail on the channel page, pinned comment at the top, Related Video link present, captions correct. | Log the check with timestamp. Any defect = fix in place, never re-upload. |
| P12 | **Log** — record in one sheet: keyword + tier, title (all variants tried) + formula used, publish time, shown-in-feed, viewed-vs-swiped-away, avg % viewed, any post-publish edit (timestamp + before/after). | Patterns across 10–20 Shorts drive the next decision, not single-video anecdotes. 72h median check date logged. |

## 11. Trend refresh — run before each batch, not every video

The old skill carried a frozen 21 Nov 2026 view-count leaderboard. Frozen leaderboards go stale within days and must never be quoted as current. Instead:

1. Run a view-count-sorted search for your game/topic + a Shorts-filter search; `--dump-json` the top 5 new entries (tool: `yt-dlp`).
2. Record title, channel, views, upload date, duration, tags, description first line. Never paste a competitor's tags or description — use the table to see which *words* searchers are using, then write fresh copy for your clip.
3. Rank what is working by **outlier multiple** (how far each video beat its own channel's median), not by raw views or channel size (adopted from yt-viral). A 300K-sub channel's 5M-view chase and a 289K-sub channel's 296K-view detail can both be outliers; only the multiple tells you which pattern travels.
4. Re-derive the Tier 1/2/3 lists from the fresh table. Time-boxed terms (launch-week, "is [game] out", download-size, falls-through-the-map style) die within 48h — post them inside the window or drop them.
5. Patterns that held in the 21 Nov 2026 snapshot (re-verify before quoting): number + promise titles; small channels winning on one sharp moment; zero tags common at the top; first lines are plain sentences; freshness window is days.
