# Purple Cow — Remarkability Scoring for Shorts

## Purpose

Decide **which moment is worth clipping and how to frame it** — before any edit, title, or thumbnail work. SEO makes a remarkable video findable; it cannot make an ordinary moment remarkable. If the idea scores below threshold, no packaging saves it: rework the angle or pick a different moment.

This is the companion to a Shorts SEO/packaging skill (titles, hooks, thumbnails, descriptions, first comments). It covers **idea selection only** and never overrides that skill's rules.

## When to Use

- Before cutting any Short: score the moment + draft title together.
- When mining a long video, stream, or VOD for clip candidates.
- When a channel's Shorts stall at low views despite good titles and hooks — the problem may be the ideas, not the packaging.
- When choosing between two candidate moments from the same source.

## Sources and Evidence Labels

- **FACT** — YouTube's own Help text or official blog, answer ID named.
- **REPORTED** — a named study or secondary source, with date, sample, and caveats. Observational, not an experiment.
- **UNVERIFIED** — no source found. Never quote as fact.
- **Recommendation** — a working rule derived from the evidence, not evidence itself.
- **TRANSCRIPT** — a direct quote from the MrBeast interview below, quoted accurately.

**Origin of the concept.** Seth Godin, *Purple Cow: Transform Your Business by Being Remarkable* (Portfolio, 2003) — be remarkable or invisible. MrBeast (Jimmy Donaldson) adopts it as his operating rule for virality: in podcast interviews he explained that a large part of getting 100M+ views on a video is whether it is something someone has seen before or not. The quotes in §1 are his wording, verified against the *Diary of a CEO* transcript (23:02, re-checked 23 Nov 2026); the 99.5s clip circulates via X post by @ryangrowthh. (Round 2 note: an earlier draft attributed the interview exclusively to Theo Von's *This Past Weekend* — the same story appears on *Diary of a CEO* with identical wording, so the show attribution is not settled; the wording is what this skill quotes.)

## 0. The Honest Tension

A Shorts SEO skill fights over search discovery and the hook fights over the feed — but neither matters if the *idea itself* is ordinary. Since 1 Oct 2026 YouTube reduces Shorts-feed reach for channels "primarily aggregating or re-uploading other creators' videos without significant changes" (FACT — TeamYouTube, 1 Oct 2026; see the companion zero-views skill). Permission and credit do **not** save such a clip. A well-edited clip of a moment anyone could have clipped is still, in the feed's eyes, a moment anyone could have clipped. The Purple Cow rule is the missing layer: **the moment itself must be something nobody has seen before.**

## 1. The Rule in One Line + the Analogy

**The rule in one line:** To go viral you have to do something that's never been done before. (TRANSCRIPT: "The thing is, like, to go viral you have to do something that's never been done before.")

**The analogy, in MrBeast's own words (TRANSCRIPT, quoted accurately):**

> "If you're driving down the road and you see a cow — who cares? It's a fucking cow. But if you're driving down the road and you see a purple cow, you're, like, you've never seen that before, and it's something you weren't expecting, and you're gonna go holy shit, and you're gonna go tell your friends about it. You're gonna remember that. You'll probably even think about it randomly once every couple of years — why the fuck was there a purple cow? And it's, like, it's the same thing, just one was a little purple."

Three things to notice — they are the whole skill:

1. **Only one thing changed** — the colour. The cow is the same cow. For a clips channel, the "cow" is a clip from your niche and the "purple" is one expectation-violating beat inside it. You do not need a different game or format; you need one beat that breaks what the viewer expected.
2. **The reaction is the test, not the difference.** A purple cow makes you stop, swear, tell your friends, and remember it years later. A merely *different* cow does not. "Different" is not the bar — *unforgettable* is.
3. **Scrolling is driving.** (TRANSCRIPT: "When you're scrolling through social media to find a video to watch, there's things that, you know, have been done before, you've seen. It's, you know, roughly similar to stuff before. You're just gonna scroll past it. You'll never think about it again. Just like you'll never think about a fucking cow on the side of the road.") The feed is a road full of cows. The viewer owes none of them a second look.

## 2. The 3 Tests — Each Is a Pass/Fail Gate

All three must pass for a video to be a purple cow. Two out of three is a strong normal cow — ship it only if it must ship, flagged as such (§4, §10). Each test is scored by the Purple Score in §3; these are the gates behind the numbers.

### Test A — Never Seen Before

- **What it is:** the exact beat — not the game, not the creator, not the activity — has not been clipped, titled, or memed before. "A car flips in [game]" fails; "the monster hugs the player back" passes, because nobody has seen *that* beat.
- **How to check it (self-test):** write the beat in one sentence. Search YouTube for that sentence's keywords + check your own catalog. If the first page of results shows the same beat from another channel, or your own channel already shipped it, it FAILS — even if your edit is better. A better edit of a seen beat is still a seen beat.
- **What it filters out:** every "first time I did X" mission clip, every standard heist/robbery/driving moment, every opening-scene reaction (everyone's first reaction to the same opening looks the same), and any moment whose title could describe ten other videos. It also filters out your own repeats: a second cryptid-style clip is not purple just because the first one was.

### Test B — "What the fuck?" Reaction

- **What it is:** the viewer's first, involuntary reaction is disbelief, not recognition. (TRANSCRIPT: "Which are things that make you go what the fuck? I've never seen that.") The beat violates an expectation the viewer walked in with.
- **How to check it (self-test):** show the title + first frame to someone cold (or read them aloud to yourself as a stranger). If their honest reaction is "oh, cool" or "yeah, that game does that", it FAILS. If it is "wait — WHAT?" followed by a question ("how?", "why?", "did that really happen?"), it PASSES. The source creator's own reaction is a proxy, not proof: a huge reaction to a *normal* beat (the opening every player sees) does not count. A big reaction to an *impossible* beat does.
- **What it filters out:** big reactions to ordinary events, loud moments with no violation in them, "funny" moments that are only funny in context, and hype words doing the work the moment should do ("INSANE", "CRAZY", "UNBELIEVABLE" on a normal driving clip). If the title needs hype words to sound surprising, the moment isn't.

### Test C — "I Have to Click or I Won't Sleep Tonight" (Open Loop)

- **What it is:** the idea opens a loop the viewer cannot close without watching. (TRANSCRIPT: "I have to click this or I'm not gonna be able to sleep tonight because, like, why is this video? No way they did this, right?") The viewer must feel that *not* clicking leaves a question unanswered.
- **How to check it (self-test):** after the title + thumbnail, write down the one question a viewer would still be asking. If the question is answerable from the title alone ("he robbed three banks" — yes, that's what it says), it FAILS. If the question can only be answered by watching ("how did the monster hug him back?", "why did ramming the sheriff earn zero stars?"), it PASSES. The loop must be *specific*: "what happens next?" is not a loop, it is a shrug. "Why did the game give him zero stars for that?" is a loop.
- **What it filters out:** titles that give the whole moment away, titles that are a summary instead of a gap, and moments with no mystery in them. It also filters out clickbait loops: a loop the video never closes (§9, gate 2) is worse than no loop at all.

## 3. Purple Score Rubric 0–10 — the Sum of 4 Sub-Scores

Score every candidate video **before** building it (§10, step 2). Score the *moment + title together*, not the moment alone — a purple moment with a normal title scores as a normal cow, because the feed never sees the moment, only the packaging. Each sub-score has explicit anchors; do not score by feel.

| Sub-score | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **N — Novelty (0–3): has this exact beat been seen before?** | Seen a hundred times — a standard mission, drive, robbery, or opening beat; or already clipped by you or anyone else. | Seen before, but not often, or not from this creator. A fresh angle on a familiar beat. | Rare — a handful of clips exist, none from this creator, none with this exact beat. | Never seen — no clip of this exact beat exists on YouTube, and it is not in your catalog. |
| **V — Expectation violation (0–3): how hard does the beat break what the viewer expected?** | No violation — the game behaved exactly as expected. | Mild surprise — unusual, but inside the game's normal rules. | Strong violation — a rule breaks, a crossover appears, or something impossible happens. | Total violation — the beat contradicts what the viewer *knows* about the game ("that can't happen"). |
| **M — Memorability / retellability (0–2): will the viewer tell a friend about it tomorrow?** | 0 — forgettable within the scroll; nothing to retell. | 1 — retellable with effort ("there was this funny clip where…"), but the detail blurs. | 2 — retellable in one sentence a day later. The one-sentence version IS the video. |
| **P — Payoff truth (0–2): does the video actually deliver what the title promises?** | 0 — the promised beat is absent, exaggerated beyond the clip, or only implied. Automatic fail (§9). | 1 — the beat is in the clip but buried, brief, or weaker than the title suggests. | 2 — the beat is clearly visible, on screen, in the first seconds, exactly as titled. |

**Total = N + V + M + P, out of 10.**

### Overall Anchors

| Total | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **0–2 — Seen it a hundred times** | A standard cow. Do not build it; pick another moment. |
| **3–4 — Familiar with a twist** | A cow with a different spot. Watchable, forgettable. Only ships as filler, flagged as a normal cow. |
| **5–6 — Unusual, not unforgettable** | A cow in an odd place. A good video, not a remarkable one. Ship if the schedule needs it; do not expect feed breakout. |
| **7–8 — Purple** | A purple cow. Never or rarely seen, a real violation, retellable in one sentence, and the payoff is on screen. This is the ship standard. |
| **9–10 — Unmistakable** | The viewer stops, swears, tells a friend, and remembers it years later. Rare — expect one or two per batch of ten, not ten per batch. If everything scores 9, the scoring is broken, not the batch. |

### Minimum Ship Threshold: 7/10

**Justification.** 7 is the lowest total at which all three tests in §2 can pass at once: it requires at least a 2 in Novelty (rare beat), a 2 in Violation (a real rule break), and a 2 in Payoff truth (the beat is actually there) — or equivalent strength elsewhere — plus at least 1 in Memorability. A 6 can pass two tests and fail the third (usually Test C: unusual but no open loop), and a 6 is exactly the profile the Oct 1 2026 update deprioritises: a decent clip of a seen beat.

**UNVERIFIED — read this before trusting the threshold.** The 7/10 threshold has **not** been validated against real view data. It is a Recommendation, set at the lowest total where all three tests can pass — not a proven cutoff, and no numeric ranking weight for remarkability in the Shorts feed is published by YouTube. No view-count prediction follows from a Purple Score; the score is a pre-publish judgement tool. Revisit the threshold after 10 scored videos, comparing scores against 30-day views. Never present 7/10 as a proven number.

**Below threshold — what happens:**

- **5–6:** rework the angle first — lead with a different beat from the same clip, re-cut to open on the violation (§6), or re-title around the strangest 3 seconds. Re-score after the rework. If it still scores 5–6 and the slot must be filled, ship it **flagged as a known normal cow** in the build note (score + which test failed), so nobody mistakes it for a breakout bet.
- **0–4:** do not ship as-is. Pick a different moment from the same source (§4). A 0–4 with a purple title is clickbait, not a purple cow, and fails §9.
- **Never lower the threshold to clear a queue.** A thin week ships one honest 6 flagged as normal; it never ships a 4 relabelled as a 7.

## 4. Idea Generation — Mining a Long Source for the One Violation

Do not start from "what should we clip today?" Start from "what broke the rules in this source?" Work in this exact order. Every step leaves a trace in the build note (source link + timestamp + score), so any score can be re-checked.

1. **Watch for the reaction spike.** Scrub the source at 2x and mark every timestamp where the creator reacts hardest — shouting, laughing, going silent, standing up, repeating a word. A reaction spike is a *candidate*, not a verdict: a huge reaction to a normal beat is still a normal beat (§2, Test B). Log the timestamp and the one-sentence beat.
2. **Watch for the impossible.** Mark every moment the game does something impossible or out-of-character: a UFO beam lifting someone, a cryptid grabbing the player, a parrot giving away a hiding place, a car on a roof, a wanted level staying at zero after a police crash. The game breaking its own rules is the most reliable purple signal, because every viewer knows the rules.
3. **Watch for a rule break.** Mark every moment a rule of the game breaks: zero stars for a crime, a truck that cannot hurt the protagonist, a shop sign carrying a real person's name, a pet that changes a stat. Ask: "would a player expect the opposite?" If yes, it is a candidate.
4. **Watch for a crossover.** Mark every moment another world walks in: a character from an older game returning, a superhero in a cave, an alien baby in a secret bunker. Crossovers are purple by construction — two things that should never share a frame, sharing one.
5. **Pick ONE beat per clip.** The clip is one violation, not three. If a source window has two candidates, they are two clips (or one clip and one spare). A clip that tries to carry two purple beats carries neither — the title cannot lead with both.
6. **Ask: "has anyone clipped THIS exact beat?"** This is Test A, run before any edit. Search YouTube for the beat's keywords. Then check your own catalog. If the exact beat is already out there — by you or anyone — it is not purple. Pick the next candidate.
7. **Score it before cutting.** Score the surviving beat with §3 (N/V/M/P) *before* cutting. If it scores below 7, go back to step 1 in the same source — a 3-hour source contains more than one candidate, and the purple one is rarely the first spike you marked. Log the score and the failed candidates (one line each) so the next miner does not re-mine them.
8. **Difficulty check (the moat).** (TRANSCRIPT: "If something's never been done before, there's usually a reason, because it's very fucking hard. So you just kind of have to train yourself to not resent very difficult, complex, hard, original problems, and actually run towards them.") If a candidate was easy to find — the first 10 minutes of a source, the opening mission, the clip every channel already posted — be suspicious of it. The purple beat is usually buried. Easy picks are picked already. Hard to find is a feature, not a cost.

### Rights and Clearance — Non-Negotiable

- Only mine and clip sources you have the right to use: your own footage, or a creator whose written permission you hold. A purple beat from an uncleared source is unusable, however high it scores.
- Credit the source creator always, on screen and in the description.
- Permission and credit protect against copyright claims; they do **nothing** for Shorts-feed distribution (companion zero-views skill, FACT Oct 1 2026). Do not present a cleared source as the reason a video will perform.

## 5. Application to TITLE — the Violation-First Formula

Build on your Shorts SEO skill's title formulas. The purple-cow twist is one change: **lead with the violation, not the activity.** Standard formulas describe what the creator *did*; a purple title describes what *should not have happened*. The game name and creator name stay, but the moment word is the violation, not the verb.

**Formula P — Violation first:** `[GAME]: [Creator] [violation in plain words] — [the impossible result]`

- Lead noun = the impossible thing (the monster, the alien baby, zero stars, the returning character), not the activity (drives, robs, fishes).
- The second half must be the result that breaks the rule ("He Hugged Back", "Zero Stars"), never a generic reaction ("was shocked", "couldn't believe it").
- Rotate Formula P with your other title formulas (never the same formula twice in a row). Formula P is an additional formula, not a replacement for the others.

**Every new title must keep:** the game name in your channel's locked form · 30–60 chars (the pack standard — see your Shorts SEO skill; a shorter specific title is fine, never pad to reach the band) · creator named · statement, not question · no "!!!", no "…", no hashtag, no emoji · and stay TRUE to the clip (the violation is in the video, §9 gate 2). Read the title aloud: if a viewer who clicked for exactly what it promises would feel shortchanged, rewrite it.

### Worked Examples — Before / After (Worked-Example Layer)

All three "before" titles are plain rewrites of the same real moment (activity-first, the way a normal cow would be titled). All three "after" titles are the purple-cow version of that same moment. Scores use §3 (N + V + M + P). Character counts verified. These examples come from one GTA VI clips channel's real clips; treat the moments as examples, not as your content.

**Example 1 — Red Hulk hug.** Source: IShowSpeed, Day 1 VOD, raw window 73,440–73,560s. Moment: Speed finds the Red Hulk in a cave and hugs him; the Hulk hugs back.

| | Title | Chars | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before (activity-first) | GTA VI: IShowSpeed Meets the Red Hulk in a Cave | 47 | **4/10** — N1 + V1 + M0 + P2 |
| After (violation-first) | GTA VI: IShowSpeed Hugged the Red Hulk — He Hugged Back | 55 | **9/10** — N2 + V3 + M2 + P2 |

Why it works: the "before" tells you what happened; the "after" tells you what should not have happened. The loop is "how does a Hulk hug someone back?" — only watchable, not answerable from the title.

**Example 2 — Zero stars.** Source: ohnePixel, Day 2 VOD, raw window 10,875–10,945s. Moment: ohnePixel crashes his coupe into the sheriff and gets ZERO stars (HUD star area empty throughout, visually confirmed).

| | Title | Chars | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before (activity-first) | GTA VI: ohnePixel Crashes Into the Sheriff | 42 | **4/10** — N1 + V1 + M0 + P2 |
| After (violation-first) | GTA VI: Nobody Expected Zero Stars for the Sheriff Crash | 56 | **8/10** — N2 + V2 + M2 + P2 |

Why it works: the "before" is a driving clip; the "after" is a rule break. The loop is "why zero stars?" — the title does not answer it, the clip must.

**Example 3 — Car on roof.** Source: Kai Cenat, Part 3 VOD, 2:42:42. Moment: Kai spots his orange car sitting on the roof of a red house in a junkyard, an excavator below.

| | Title | Chars | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before (activity-first) | GTA VI: Kai Cenat Finds a Car in the Junkyard | 45 | **3/10** — N1 + V0 + M0 + P2 |
| After (violation-first) | GTA VI: Kai Cenat's Car Ended Up on the Roof | 44 | **7/10** — N2 + V2 + M1 + P2 |

Why it works — and why it only just passes: the violation is real but small (a physics glitch, not a crossover or a rule break with stakes). 7/10 is the floor, not the goal; a batch needs its 8s and 9s from the bigger beats, not from glitches alone.

**The pattern to avoid:** "GTA VI: Speed's Car Flips Mid-Air" (33 chars) is a normal-cow title twice over — it leads with the activity (a flip) and it is under the 40-char floor. A purple version would lead with the violation in that flip (what the car landed on, what it survived, who was watching) and would need the clip to actually contain it.

## 6. Application to HOOK (First 3 Seconds) — Open on the Violation Itself

Your SEO skill already says: no logo, no establishing shot, no "welcome back"; open mid-action. The purple-cow addition: **the first frame IS the violation.** The setup (where the creator was, what they were doing, how they got there) is the price of the clip, not the opening. Three patterns:

1. **Open on the impossible outcome.** First frame: the thing that should not be happening, already happening. No context first. *Example:* open on the Red Hulk's arms closing around Speed — the hug-back — before any shot of Speed walking into the cave. The viewer arrives mid-hug and has to stay to learn how they got there.
2. **Open on the rule breaking.** First frame: the evidence that a rule just broke — the HUD, the number, the empty stars — before the action that caused it. *Example:* open on the empty wanted-star area while the coupe is still smoking from the sheriff crash. The viewer sees "zero stars" before they see the crash, and the loop is open from frame one.
3. **Open on the crossover.** First frame: the two things that should never share a frame, already sharing it. *Example:* open on Niko Bellic's face in the GTA VI cutscene — not on the creator's facecam, not on the mission setup. A GTA IV character in a GTA VI frame is the violation; the payoff follows.

**Hook self-test:** mute the clip, watch only the first 2 seconds at thumb distance. If the violation is not visible in those 2 seconds, the hook is a normal-cow hook — re-cut so it is.

## 7. Application to THUMBNAIL — One Visual Violation + One Hook Line

Where Shorts use auto thumbnails, the first frame IS the thumbnail in the feed, so §6 does the thumbnail's job. When custom thumbnails are available, the rule is: **one visual violation + one hook line (max ~5 words), from that video's actual moment; it must extend the title, not duplicate it.**

- **One visual violation:** the single frame that shows the impossible thing — the monster's arms around the player, the glowing baby on the gurney, the empty star area. One focal point; a facecam reaction, where one exists, takes roughly a quarter to a third of the frame.
- **One hook line, max ~5 words:** the violation in the fewest words, in different words from the title. It answers "what am I looking at?" and opens the same loop the title opened — it never repeats the title's sentence.
- **Extend, don't duplicate:** if the title says the Hulk hugged back, the thumbnail line must not say "HE HUGGED BACK". It must add the detail the title left out.

| Video (worked example) | Title (for contrast) | Thumbnail hook line (≤5 words) | Visual violation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red Hulk hug | GTA VI: IShowSpeed Hugged the Red Hulk — He Hugged Back | HE HUGGED THE RED HULK (5) | The Hulk's arms closing around Speed |
| Alien baby | GTA VI: Nobody Expected Windah to Steal an Alien Baby | HE STOLE AN ALIEN BABY (5) | The glowing baby on the gurney, red lights |
| Sheriff crash | GTA VI: Nobody Expected Zero Stars for the Sheriff Crash | ZERO STARS FOR SHERIFF CRASH (5) | The empty wanted-star HUD over the smoking coupe |

**Gate:** if the thumbnail line could belong to a different video, it is too generic. "HE HUGGED THE RED HULK" belongs to exactly one video in the world. "INSANE GTA MOMENT" belongs to all of them — never use it.

## 8. Application to FIRST COMMENT — Ask the Question the Purple Cow Raises

Your SEO skill already says: a specific question beats a generic CTA. The purple-cow addition: **the comment asks the "why was there a purple cow?" question — the question the violation itself raises — not a generic verdict question.** A verdict question ("was it the worst ending ever?") works for any video; a purple-cow question works for exactly one video, because only that video raised it. Post at publish +60 min (see §11 example rule; set your own timing and keep it consistent); no link, no pitch.

| Video (worked example) | Generic verdict question (do NOT use) | Purple-cow question (use) |
|---|---|---|
| UFO beam lifts a woman in the night woods while the protagonist watches | Was this the craziest UFO moment ever? | Why would a UFO beam someone up in Leonida — and why was the protagonist just standing there watching it? |
| A red macaw squawks "Lot of cash! Lot of cash!" and gives away where the money is | Was the parrot the best part of the video? | If a parrot told you where the money was hidden, would you trust it — or is the bird setting a trap? |
| The protagonist stands in traffic as a semi-truck bears down and walks away unharmed | Is the game too easy? | A semi-truck hit her head-on and she walked away — is the game broken, or is she? |

**Test:** if the comment question still makes sense with a different video's title swapped in, it is generic. Rewrite it until it only makes sense for this clip.

## 9. Hard Gates — a Failed Gate Stops the Publish

These sit alongside (not instead of) your SEO skill's checklist and your description skill's gates. A failed gate here stops the publish the same way a failed description gate does.

| # | Gate | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Must be TRUE** | The violation is in the clip, traceable (source link + raw window + trim). No beat without a timestamp. If the verify sheet cannot point at it, it does not exist. |
| 2 | **Must be IN the video** | The payoff is visible, not promised and absent. The title's violation appears on screen, in the first seconds where possible (§6). A viewer clicking for exactly what the title promises must not feel shortchanged (FACT — Help: a title must accurately represent the video). |
| 3 | **Must be remarkable, not random** | Weirdness with no payoff fails. A random glitch, a misclick, a 2-second visual bug with no reaction and no consequence is not a purple cow — it is noise. The violation must matter inside the clip: someone reacts to it, it changes what happens next, or it breaks a rule the viewer cares about. |
| 4 | **Difficulty check** | If it was easy to find, someone already did it — be suspicious of easy picks (§4, step 8). A candidate from the first 10 minutes of a source, the opening mission, or the clip every channel posted needs a second, harder look. If the only reason it scores 7 is that nobody bothered to title it this way, it is a 5 with good copy. |
| 5 | **No fabrication** | No invented moment, number, or quote. No "3 seconds" unless the clip shows ~3 seconds. No quoted word unless it was actually said in the clip, transcript-verified. No invented view count, earning, or testimonial anywhere. |

## 10. Workflow — Idea → Score → Build → Verify → Publish

| Step | Action | Gate (a failed gate stops the step) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Idea** | Mine a cleared source by §4 (reaction spike → impossible → rule break → crossover); pick ONE beat; log source link + timestamp. | Cleared source only (§4). No timestamp, no idea. |
| 2. **Score** | Run Tests A/B/C (§2) and the Purple Score (§3) on the moment + draft title together. Log N/V/M/P and the total. | Total **≥7/10** to build. 5–6: rework the angle (§3) or pick another beat. 0–4: do not build. |
| 3. **Build** | Cut the layout opening on the violation (§6); write the Formula P title (§5) + description + fresh tags + thumbnail line (§7) + first comment (§8). | Title: game name, 30–60 chars, creator named, statement, true to the clip. Description: your description skill's gates. Tags: fresh, both spellings where they exist, channel tags last. |
| 4. **Verify** | Re-check against the verify sheet: the violation is at the logged timestamp, the quoted words match the transcript, the numbers match the clip, the first frame shows the violation (muted 2-second test). | Hard gates §9, all five. Any fail = fix the cut or the copy, re-score, re-verify. Never ship a failed gate to "fix after publish". |
| 5. **Publish** | Upload on your fixed schedule (see §11 example: 1/day). First comment at publish +60 min. Read back title/description/tags/playlist/schedule from the details page (never the list view). Log the formula used, the Purple Score, and the 30-day check date. | SEO checklist + description gates + §9 hard gates, all green. If a copy error is found after publish, edit in place — never delete and re-upload to fix copy. |

## 11. Example-Channel Rules — One Channel's Locked Rules, Labelled as Examples

The rules below are **one GTA VI clips channel's own locked rules**, reproduced as a worked example of how to lock a channel's conventions. They are NOT universal rules. Replace every bracketed value with your own channel's decisions and lock those instead.

- **[GAME NAME FORM]** — Example: every title says "GTA VI", never "GTA 6". Descriptions/tags keep mixed "GTA 6 / GTA VI" wording for search.
- **[DESCRIPTION FORMAT]** — Example: every description passes a 13-gate checklist before upload; locked subscribe line "Subscribe for a new [GAME] clip every day."; credit on its own line; a disclaimer + contact block after the subscribe line, before hashtags. Live videos are never edited for a copy fix.
- **[ONE-CREATOR-PER-VIDEO]** — Example: a description mentions ONLY the creator whose clip it is; no other creator, no other name. Each video goes into that creator's playlist only.
- **[FRESH TAGS]** — Example: before every upload, search the most-searched/viral tags for the topic and use them in the description AND the Tags field. Content tags are FRESH every time — never repeat a video's content tags. Every video carries 2–3 channel-name tags — the one intentional repeat.
- **[FIRST-COMMENT TIMING]** — Example: the first comment is posted **1 HOUR after the video goes public — never 5 minutes after.**
- **[SCHEDULE]** — Example: 1 strong Short/day, 20:00 [TIMEZONE]. No batch dumps.
- **[NO FABRICATION]** — Permanent, universal: no invented views, earnings, testimonials, moments, numbers, or quotes anywhere in titles, descriptions, tags, or comments.
- **[CLEARED CREATORS ONLY]** — Example: clip freely (credit always) only creators with written permission on file. Never clip a creator who has refused. A named never-clip list is kept and checked at step 1 of every workflow.
