# Platform Policy, Monetization & Disclosure

## Purpose

Keep every post inside current platform policy: correct AI disclosures, honest monetisation claims, proper affiliate/sponsor disclosure, and no policy claim stated without a re-check. Policies change fast — this skill treats every rule as dated evidence, not permanent truth.

**This skill is general information, not legal advice.** For paid campaigns, regulated topics (health, finance, legal), or any post where a wrong call could cost money or an account, get a qualified professional to review it.

## When to use

- Before any post that mentions income, monetisation, virality, or platform eligibility.
- Before any post using realistic AI-generated people, voices, images, or video.
- Before any affiliate/sponsored mention, gifted product, or paid partnership.
- Before each publishing cycle: verify each platform's latest official creator guidance.
- After a strike, a demonetisation, a label applied by the platform, or a rejected monetisation application.

## When NOT to use

- As a substitute for reading the official policy page linked in §12. This skill tells you what to check and where; the official page is the authority when they disagree.
- For legal questions (contracts, copyright fair use, defamation). Those need a lawyer, not a checklist.

## Rules (hard)

- **Never say AI videos automatically qualify for monetisation, or guarantee income, views, or virality.** No platform promises any of those.
- **Monetisation posts re-check current official eligibility** (originality, reused/inauthentic content, disclosure) before publishing — never from memory.
- **Realistic synthetic content:** check the latest disclosure rules per platform and enable the required label (§2–§5). "Realistic" is the trigger on every platform; a cartoon, a colour grade, or a caption is not.
- **Affiliate/sponsor disclosure:** clear, upfront, in the post itself; rankings stay independent; sponsorship never determines a ranking.
- **If a policy claim is uncertain, label it, investigate more, or do not publish.** An UNVERIFIED claim never ships as fact.
- **If an account is not connected, mark analytics/access unavailable — do not claim it is live.** Never present a platform you cannot open as verified.
- **A platform label is not a legal disclosure on its own.** The FTC position (§7) is that a platform toggle alone may not be enough — add your own plain-language words too.

## Evidence labels (used throughout)

- **FACT** — read on the platform's or regulator's own page on the date shown.
- **REPORTED** — a named secondary source with a date; credible, but not the official page. Re-check before quoting.
- **UNVERIFIED** — no source found, or the official page could not be read. Never quote as fact; the skill says so in the open.

**Re-check rule:** every FACT in this skill carries a "verified" date. Before you rely on it for a real post, open the linked official page again. If the page changed, the page wins and this skill is updated, not the other way round.

## 1. The one-line test (run on every post, 30 seconds)

1. **Is anything in this post realistic but not real?** (A person saying something they did not say, a place/event that did not happen, a realistic scene that never occurred, AI music as the main focus.) → If yes, §2–§5: label it on that platform.
2. **Does this post claim money, eligibility, or virality?** → If yes, §6 + the official eligibility page, re-checked today. No guarantees, ever.
3. **Did anyone pay, gift, discount, or commission anything in this post?** → If yes, §7: disclose it in the post itself, in plain words, where it cannot be missed.

If all three answers are "no", the post still gets the §11 checklist — it takes two minutes and it is the only thing standing between a post and a strike.

## 2. YouTube — disclosure, monetisation, originality

### 2.1 AI disclosure (FACT — YouTube Help "Disclosing use of GenAI content", answer 14328491, read 2026-11-23)

YouTube **requires** creators to disclose AI-generated or altered content **that seems realistic**. Disclose in YouTube Studio during upload: Attributes → "AI use" → Yes/No.

You **must** disclose content that:

- Makes a real person appear to say or do something they did not say or do.
- Alters footage of a real event or place.
- Generates a realistic scene that did not actually occur.
- Creates music that is the main focus of the video.

You do **not** need to disclose: AI used for scripts, titles, thumbnails, captions, or idea generation; non-realistic or minor edits (colour correction, cropping, filters). Realistic content and meaningful changes require disclosure; non-realistic or minor edits do not.

**Labels:** a "Yes" answer adds a label — on the video player for photorealistic content or AI music, in the expanded description for non-photorealistic or animated content. YouTube states that **disclosing AI content alone will not limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money**.

**Automatic labels:** YouTube may apply a label itself for content made with YouTube's GenAI tools, content carrying C2PA metadata, or content its systems detect as AI-generated. You will be notified in Studio. You can usually change an incorrect auto-label back to "No" — except for content made with YouTube's AI tools, content with C2PA metadata, or content labelled after manual review.

**Risks of not disclosing:** creators who consistently do not disclose may face manual labelling, content removal, or YouTube Partner Program suspension.

### 2.2 Partner Program eligibility (FACT — YouTube Help "YouTube Partner Program overview & eligibility", answer 72851, read 2026-11-23)

To join YPP a channel needs: follow the channel monetisation policies; be in a country/region where YPP is available; no active Community Guidelines strikes; 2-Step Verification on; advanced-features access; one active AdSense for YouTube account linked.

Eligibility thresholds (either route):

1. **1,000 subscribers + 4,000 qualified watch hours in the last 12 months**, or
2. **1,000 subscribers + 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days.**

Qualified watch hours count only from public long-form videos — Shorts Feed views do **not** count toward the 4,000 hours. Meeting the threshold does not guarantee acceptance: every channel goes through a standard review (typically about 1 month). If rejected, you can appeal within 21 days or re-apply after 30 days (90 days after a repeat rejection). Monetisation may be switched off if a channel has not uploaded or posted for 6 months or more.

**REPORTED, NOT on the official page — do not quote as fact:** in August 2026 several creator-news sources (vidIQ, Matt Navarra) reported YouTube will double the thresholds for **new** applicants from **1 February 2027** (8,000 watch hours / 20 million Shorts views), with existing partners grandfathered. As of 2026-11-23 the official Help page above still shows 4,000 / 10 million. Treat the doubling as unconfirmed until the official page changes — re-check answer 72851 before any post that mentions it.

### 2.3 Monetisation policies — what actually blocks earning (FACT — YouTube Help "Channel monetization policies", answer 1311392, read 2026-11-23)

These policies apply to anyone in, or applying to, YPP, and reviewers judge the **channel as a whole** (main theme, most-viewed videos, newest videos, biggest share of watch time, metadata, About section).

**Reused content.** Channels that repurpose content already on YouTube or another source **without adding significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value** cannot monetise it. YouTube's test is whether viewers can tell there is a meaningful difference between the original and yours. **This policy applies even if you have permission from the original creator, and it is separate from copyright — it is not based on copyright, permission, or fair use.** Allowed: clips used for a critical review; a movie scene with rewritten dialogue and changed voiceover; sports replays where you explain the moves; reaction videos where you comment; edited footage where you add a storyline and commentary. Not allowed: show moments edited together with little or no narrative; short videos compiled from other social media; song collections even with permission; content uploaded many times by other creators; mostly non-verbal reactions without voice commentary; readings of other people's material; songs with only pitch/speed changed.

**Inauthentic content** (renamed from "repetitious content", 15 July 2025 — no change to the reused-content policy). Mass-produced, generic, or repetitive content is ineligible: templated videos, image slideshows or scrolling text with minimal narrative, and "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective."

**Unsatisfying or off-putting content.** Content that relies on emotionally manipulative formulas or shock without a cohesive narrative is ineligible — including videos that stitch unrelated AI clips together to surprise viewers, and realistic visuals tricking viewers into believing a fake celebrity death or natural disaster occurred.

**AI personas on sensitive topics.** Channels that use AI-generated personas to present themselves as human experts giving advice on health, legal, finance, or politics **cannot monetise** — e.g. an AI "doctor" giving diagnoses, AI podcast hosts giving investment tips, AI personas interpreting laws.

**Shorts originality (FACT — YouTube Community, 1 Oct 2026):** recommendations prioritise original content and reduce reach for content re-uploaded from other creators "without adding anything of your own". A credit line does not satisfy this — transformation must be in the video.

## 3. Meta — Facebook, Instagram, Threads

### 3.1 AI labelling (FACT — Meta, "Our Approach to Labeling AI-Generated Content and Manipulated Media", Apr 2024 with dated updates, read via Meta pages Oct–Nov 2026)

- Meta **requires** people posting organic content with **photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered** to use its AI disclosure tool. Meta says it **may apply penalties** if they do not. The rule covers Facebook, Instagram, and Threads. Viewers see the label as **"AI info"** (renamed from "Made with AI" on 1 July 2024).
- Still images are **not** named in the self-disclosure duty. Meta labels them itself when it detects industry-standard signals: C2PA / IPTC metadata and invisible watermarks. Content only *edited* with AI tools (rather than generated) gets the "AI info" label in the post's menu, not on the post itself.
- Meta keeps labelled content up and adds context, unless it violates another policy. High-risk deception may get a more prominent label.
- **Ads:** advertisers **must** disclose AI use in ads about social issues, elections, or politics (photorealistic image/video or realistic audio created or edited with AI). For other ads, Meta labels automatically when its own generative tools are used or metadata is detected.
- **REPORTED (Instagram for Creators, Aug–Sep 2026):** profiles built around an AI-generated person carry an **"AI-generated profile"** label (renamed from a May 2026 "AI creator" test). Profiles that add the label keep their reach; unlabelled ones may see limits. Creators who only use AI tools in their process do not need it.

### 3.2 Originality — Facebook (FACT — Meta Newsroom, "Rewarding Original Creators on Facebook", 13 Mar 2026, read 2026-11-23)

- **Original** on Facebook: content filmed or produced directly by the creator or the owner of the Profile/Page.
- **Remixes/overlays** count as original only when the focus is an on-screen creator presence presenting something genuinely new — fresh information, analysis, or substantial improvements to a storyline.
- **Unoriginal (deprioritised in Feed and Reels):** simply watching along, reacting with facial expressions, stitching multiple clips together, or narrating what is already on screen without adding anything meaningful; duplicative content or minor edits to someone else's post (adding borders, captions, changing speed). Creators who keep posting primarily unoriginal content may see their account deemed **non-recommendable and demonetised**. Appeals are available.
- Scale Meta cites: original Reels views and watch time approximately doubled in H2 2025 vs H2 2024; 20M+ accounts impersonating large creators removed in 2025.

### 3.3 Paid partnership label (REPORTED — Meta/Instagram Help pages via independent reads, Oct–Nov 2026)

Instagram's Branded Content Policies require the **"Paid partnership with [brand]"** label whenever a brand paid you — including free products. How to add it (app only, professional account, branded-content tools enabled): post/reel → Next → More options → Partnership label and ads → turn on **Add paid partnership label** → add the brand (up to two). Story: tag icon → Add paid partnership label. Live: Details → Add brand partner, before going live only. The brand must approve the first tag; until then the label shows without the brand name. **FTC position (§7): the platform label alone is not enough — add your own plain-language disclosure in the caption too.**

## 4. TikTok

### 4.1 AI-generated content (FACT — TikTok Help Center, "About AI-generated content", read Nov 2026)

- **Definition:** images, video, or audio generated or modified by AI, including realistic human likenesses and stylised depictions (painting, cartoons, anime).
- **Required:** creators **must** label all AI-generated content that contains **realistic images, audio, or video**.
- **Encouraged:** labelling content that was completely generated or significantly edited by AI — e.g. the primary subject shown doing something they did not do (dancing), saying something they did not say (AI speech), or altered until unrecognisable (face-swap).
- **How:** the creator label toggle when posting (viewers see "Creator labeled as AI-generated"), or text / a hashtag sticker / context in the description. TikTok says adding the label **will not affect the distribution of your video**, and you cannot remove the label after posting.
- **Auto label:** TikTok may label content itself when you use TikTok's AI effects or upload content carrying C2PA Content Credentials. An auto label **cannot be removed**.
- **If you skip it:** TikTok may remove content that violates its Community Guidelines by depicting misleading information or unlabeled AI-generated content. Some AI content is banned label or not (e.g. content that could seriously mislead on public issues or harm a real person).
- **Ads:** an ad that is completely AI-generated or significantly modified by AI needs the "This ad contains AI-generated content" toggle in Ads Manager (disclaimer shows for the duration of the ad). Duplicating a campaign resets the toggle — re-tick it on every copy. Insignificant edits (lighting, brightness, colour saturation, background removal, denoise) need no label.

## 5. X (Twitter)

X is the outlier: there is **no general requirement to label AI-generated content** and no creator-facing AI disclosure toggle in its published rules (checked Oct 2026). What exists instead is a **harm test** (REPORTED — X Help Center Authenticity policy, via independent reads Oct 2026):

- **Synthetic and manipulated media:** you may not deceptively share synthetic or manipulated media **likely to cause harm**. Both parts matter — deceptive AND harmful. A stylised fake nobody could act on is usually fine on this rule; a fabricated clip of a real person or event that people might act on is not. X may label such posts, make them less visible, or remove them.
- **AI text is not penalised.** X's rules never call AI-written text a violation. What they penalise is behaviour AI makes easy: bulk duplicate posts, unsolicited automated replies, spam.
- **AI reply bots need prior written approval from X.** Deploying or operating an AI-powered automated reply bot without it violates the automation rules; you are responsible for everything your account (or apps connected to it) does.
- **"Made with AI" labels** on X posts come from the poster flagging AI media or X detecting it from the file (REPORTED, 2026). Treat any X label as a signal, not a rule you can rely on.
- Because X's rule is harm-based, the safe practice is the same as everywhere else: label realistic AI media in the post itself, and never present a synthetic clip of a real person or event as real.

## 7. The law layer — FTC endorsement and disclosure rules (US)

Platform rules sit on top of the law. The FTC rules below apply to US audiences and to anyone endorsing to US consumers; other countries have their own equivalents (UK CAP Code / CMA, EU consumer law). This section is general information, not legal advice.

### 7.1 Endorsement Guides (FACT — FTC, 16 CFR Part 255, revised 29 June 2023; "Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers")

- **Material connection:** disclose any connection your audience would not expect that could affect how much weight they give your opinion — payment in any form, free or discounted products (even unsolicited, even if you were not asked to post), affiliate commissions, employment, and personal or family ties to the brand. Tagging a brand counts as an endorsement.
- **"Clear and conspicuous":** the 2023 revision defines it as **difficult to miss and easily understandable by ordinary consumers** — and in interactive media (including social platforms) the disclosure must be **unavoidable**.
- **Where it must sit:** in the post itself, with the endorsement. The FTC names the places disclosures are likely to be missed: on an About/profile page only; at the end of a post or video; anywhere a reader has to click "more"; mixed into a block of hashtags or links.
- **Platform tools are not enough on their own.** The FTC says: do not assume a platform's disclosure tool is good enough — the creator is ultimately responsible for clearly disclosing.
- **Video:** put the disclosure in the video, in audio **and** on screen together (many viewers watch muted), not only in the description.
- **Words that pass:** "ad", "advertisement", "sponsored", "#ad", "#sponsored", "Thanks to [brand] for the free product", "[Brand]Partner". **Words that fail:** #sp, #spon, #collab, "thanks" on its own, "ambassador" on its own, any other shorthand.

### 7.2 Fake reviews and testimonials (FACT — FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule, 16 CFR Part 465, in force 21 Oct 2024)

Bans fake reviews and testimonials, buying fake followers, and paying for reviews based on sentiment. This is a binding rule with civil penalties — unlike the Guides, which are interpretations of the FTC Act.

### 7.3 Penalties (REPORTED — verify the current figure before quoting)

The FTC's maximum civil penalty was **$53,088 per violation** (2025 inflation-adjusted figure; each undisclosed post counts as a separate violation). One source reports the scheduled 2026 inflation adjustment was cancelled, leaving $53,088 current — **verify on the FTC's penalty page before quoting any figure.**

### 7.4 EU AI Act transparency (REPORTED)

Article 50 transparency duties have applied since **2 August 2026** and reach creators outside the EU when their output is used in the Union: AI-generated content that could be mistaken for real (including deepfakes) must be disclosed as artificially generated. Verify against the official EU text before relying on this in a post.

## 9. Monetisation eligibility decision tree

Answer in order. A "no" anywhere is a stop — fix it before you apply or before you claim eligibility.

```
1. Is the channel/account yours, with no active strikes? ──No──▶ Resolve the strike first.
2. Do you meet the numeric threshold (YouTube §2.2; other platforms: check their current creator-program page)? ──No──▶ Do not claim eligibility; keep building.
3. Is the content original and authentic? (Would the video make sense without any borrowed clip? Is the structure different from your last five videos? Is there a visible point of view?) ──No──▶ See §10 — rework the catalogue before applying.
4. Is any reused material transformed (commentary, storyline, substantive edit) rather than reposted? ──No──▶ Reused-content rejection risk (§2.3).
5. Is realistic AI content disclosed, and are AI personas kept off sensitive topics (health / legal / finance / politics advice)? ──No──▶ Fix disclosure; drop or rework the persona content.
6. Are affiliate/sponsor relationships disclosed in-post, in plain words? ──No──▶ Add the disclosure (§7, §10).
7. All yes ──▶ Apply / publish, and record the re-check date in the log (§11).
```

## 10. Disclosure wording templates (copy, fill in, do not reword the operative words)

**Caption — affiliate link (any platform):**
> I earn a commission if you buy through the link below — it costs you nothing extra. [Brand] did not pay me to say this; the recommendation is my own.

**Caption — gifted product:**
> [Brand] sent me this product for free. This post is not sponsored, and [Brand] did not approve what I say about it.

**Caption — paid sponsorship (first line of the caption, before "more"):**
> Paid partnership with [Brand] — [Brand] paid me to make this post.

**On-screen — video (first 3 seconds, large text, stays on screen while the product is shown):**
> AD — Paid by [Brand]

**Spoken — video (say it, do not rely on the caption):**
> "[Brand] paid me to make this video" / "I earn a commission from the links below."

**AI content — caption or on-screen (where the platform has no toggle, or in addition to it):**
> Created with AI: this [image/video/voice] is AI-generated and does not show a real [person/event].

**Ranking independence (any ranked list with a sponsor or affiliate):**
> Rankings are my own. Sponsorship does not change the order.

**What never counts as a disclosure:** #sp, #collab, "thanks [brand]", a disclosure only in your bio, a disclosure after the "more" fold, a disclosure only in a platform toggle, a disclosure only at the end of a video.

## 11. Originality rules summary (the two policies that decide most rejections)

**YouTube (§2.3):** original + authentic; reused content needs significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational/entertainment value; permission does not save it; judged on the channel as a whole; templated/mass-produced AI content is "inauthentic" and ineligible; AI personas giving health/legal/finance/politics advice cannot monetise.

**Meta (§3.2):** original = filmed/produced by you; remixes count only with on-screen presence + genuinely new information/analysis/storyline; watching along, facial reactions, stitching clips, narrating the screen, or minor edits (borders, captions, speed) = unoriginal and deprioritised; repeat offenders risk non-recommendable + demonetised.

**The shared test (use it on every video before upload):** if you removed everything you did not make, would anything worth watching remain? If the answer is no, the video is a re-upload with decoration — rework it or do not publish it as original.

## 12. Pre-publish checklist + re-check cadence

### 12.1 Per-post checklist (gates — a failed gate stops the publish)

- [ ] AI-generated/altered realistic content identified → required label enabled on that platform (§8)?
- [ ] Monetisation/income claim present? → official policy re-checked today + no guarantee language (§2.2, §2.3)?
- [ ] Affiliate/sponsor/gift present? → disclosure written in plain words, in the post itself, before the fold (§7, §10) + platform label enabled?
- [ ] Ranking present? → independence stated; sponsorship did not set the order (§10)?
- [ ] Originality: would the post stand without borrowed material? (§11)?
- [ ] Platform-specific disclosure rules verified for EACH platform the post ships to (§8)?
- [ ] No income/view/virality guarantee anywhere in copy, on-screen text, or thumbnail?

### 12.2 Re-check cadence

- **Every post:** §12.1, same day.
- **Every publishing cycle:** open the official pages in §13 and confirm nothing moved.
- **Monthly:** re-read the YouTube monetisation policies page and the Meta AI-labelling page; log the date.
- **Immediately:** after any platform announcement, strike, label, or rejection — re-verify the affected rule before the next post.
